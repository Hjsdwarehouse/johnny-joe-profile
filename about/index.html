01

Where I come from

Hongji Shunda has been making steel buildings since 2000. Today we work from a 107,000 m² manufacturing base with six production workshops. Most of what goes wrong in an exported steel building is not the steel — it is a detail that was never resolved on paper before the container was loaded.

02

What I actually do

Buyers describe a business, not a structure. A food processor in Brazil needs a plant; a cooperative in Chile needs grain storage; a logistics operator in Mozambique needs a freight warehouse. My job is to turn that sentence into a building: span and column spacing, eave height, roof and wall system, crane and mezzanine provision, insulation strategy, and the corrosion protection that suits the local environment. Wind loading is calculated for the location of the project — never assumed, and never a figure picked because it sounds safe.

03

How I work

Our detailing is done in Tekla, so the drawings are clash-free before fabrication starts. Every part is marked and packed in a planned sequence, and first-time builders receive a 3D installation guide. I stay with the project through fabrication and shipment, and I would rather answer a difficult question early than explain a problem late.

04

Why buyers keep talking to me

I answer directly. If a design will not survive the local climate, I say so. If something is outside what we do well, I say that too. Clients in Africa and South America keep coming back for the same reason: what they were promised on the drawing is what stands on site.
