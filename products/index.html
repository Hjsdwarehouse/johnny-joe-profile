00 / Product lines

Four lines, four different first question

A warehouse is decided by the racking. A factory is decided by the crane. A bridge is decided by the splice. A heavy structure is decided by the piece that has to be lifted. Pick the one closest to your project.

  • Steel warehouse buildings — A warehouse is a racking layout with a roof over it. Span, bay spacing and eave height are decided by the storage system and the trucks — this page is about which number moves which part of the frame.
  • Factory and workshop buildings — A production building is defined by what hangs from the frame and what crosses the floor: crane capacity, mezzanine loads and process layout decide the structure before the floor area does.
  • Steel bridge structures — A bridge is a fatigue structure before it is a span. Girder depth, splice position and the connection detail decide how it behaves under repeated loading — and all three are drawn before a plate is cut.
  • Heavy structural steel — Heavy steelwork is decided by the piece that has to be lifted, not by the floor area. Section weight, plate thickness and the erection weight of a single member set the crane, the splice and the shop sequence.
